What is Cancer?

All cancers originate from our normal cells. Most cancers arise from our interaction with the world in our daily lives. In most cases, cancer can be caused by an emotional trauma, such as losing a child, or by extended emotional stress. Cancer can be caused by issues with teeth, specifically root canals and/or cavitations. These experiences suppress the immune system and provide an opportunistic condition for cancer to grow. Cancer can also be caused by what we drink, smoke and eat. Many cooked (especially microwaved) foods have no live enzymes and few nutrients.

Cancer means the growth of tumors. About 90% of cancers involve a tumor, however not all tumors are cancerous. Cancer also includes a broad range of diseases where tumors are not present. These include lymph system cancers called lymphoma, blood-borne cancers called leukemia, and skin cancers called melanoma.

Cancer is usually a reaction to the lifestyle choices you have made and what has happened to you over the last several years.

96–97% of cancers are caused by a breakdown in cellular communication. If your body’s immune system is healthy enough to fight off all the toxins and free radicals, you don’t get the reaction called cancer. Only 3 to 4% of all cancers come from inherited genes.
